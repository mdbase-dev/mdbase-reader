Durable evidence

Durable evidence is material preserved with its original context and structure. This synthetic encyclopedia article demonstrates the relationships between source text, references, and data tables that a reader must retain.

Background

A reading copy supports later research when its paragraphs remain available. Extraction must distinguish navigation from substantive passages, even when a passage is short or includes several links to references.

We preserve a citation exactly as captured[1] so the footnote and its original anchor remain connected. Citations should not be rewritten into new numbering schemes when the surrounding prose can be preserved.

Measurements

Data tables provide a compact account of the observations. Each row belongs to a different condition; extracting only the prose would remove important evidence even though the remaining word count might appear sufficient.
